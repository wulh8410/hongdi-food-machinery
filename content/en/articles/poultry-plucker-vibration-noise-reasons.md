## Direct Answer
Start by checking whether the floor is level and whether the batch is overloaded. Then check rubber fingers, belts, motor fixation, and bearings.

## Check Usage Conditions First
An uneven floor, unstable machine feet, overloading, or birds with very different weights can increase noise and vibration. Many site problems come from placement and feeding rhythm rather than machine damage.

## Check Wear Parts
Uneven rubber finger wear, loose belts, worn bearings, or loose motor bolts may cause abnormal sound. For machines used for a long time, a short no-load test helps identify changes.

## When to Stop the Machine
Stop immediately if there is impact noise, burning smell, abnormal speed, strong shaking, or motor overheating. Continuing to run may enlarge the damage.

## Buying Points
- Stable frame structure
- Firm machine feet
- Even rubber finger layout
- Easy maintenance for motor and transmission parts
- Spare parts and support from the manufacturer
